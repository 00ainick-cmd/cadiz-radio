# Historical research — source of truth

Lineage is the home of all of Nick's historical research. Trees, town microhistories, Wayside places. One drawer.

## What Lineage holds

- Family trees. Nick's Brown tree is the demo dataset, not a private diary.
- Microhistories of places. Cadiz, Harrison County, Ohio. Other towns ancestors lived in.
- Wayside locations. Georgetown first. Other doors when Nick opens them.

If it is not in Lineage, it did not happen as research.

## What a claim still needs

A place or person in Lineage is not true because the app stored it. It is true because a source said it, and that source is filed.

For Cadiz 1844 the printed Sentinel is the voice (Chronicling America, LCCN sn84028793). A transcription is a reading copy. Radio reads the transcription. Nothing is rewritten.

A later map or history keeps its year: Jarvis 1862, Caldwell 1875, Sanborn 1887, Hanna, McGavran, the 1893 courthouse, the 198-by-198 square. Lineage already has place-source slots for atlas_1862, atlas_1875, and sanborn. Use them. Never let a later map silently become 1844 rooms.

The 1844 courthouse is the 1818 brick (42 feet square). The jail and sheriff’s house are the 1819 Olmstead pile. Unlocated stays unlocated. Do not guess a coordinate.

Cadiz is a town microhistory. It names people the paper named. It is not a hunt for Brown or Chaney lines.

## What the other piles are

- **Genesis** (`50-Genealogy/Cadiz-Sentinel/` and the rest of the vault) is the second brain for specs and drafts Nick reviews. It is not the historical register.
- **cadiz-radio** is a view of the voice.
- **cadiz-walk** is a view. An impression of Market Street, not a surveyed plat.
- **Wayside** is a view. Cadiz stays locked there until Nick opens it. Georgetown is the first door.
- **The box, GitHub, localhost** are working copies of views.

The Lineage app already has People, Sources, Places, a map, place types (street, building, hotel, business), and map layers. File Cadiz and Georgetown there. Do not start a second register.
